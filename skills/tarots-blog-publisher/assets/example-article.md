## What does The Tower mean in the Thoth Tarot?

Aleister Crowley called the card "The Blasted Tower" in *The Book of Thoth*: an outdated construction is struck by
a flash of lightning, and what falls was already unstable. The image is sudden, but the causes usually are not. Read
it as a moment of clarity that arrives with force, the point where denial stops working.

In the Golden Dawn system the card belongs to the Hebrew letter Peh, path 27 on the Tree of Life between Netzach
and Hod, and to Mars. That is the energy of a blade: fast, cutting, impersonal. Compare it with [[card:the-star]],
the card that so often follows it in a story of recovery.

## The Tower upright and ill-dignified

The Thoth Tarot has no reversed cards, so the useful contrast is between the card well-dignified, where the collapse
is a liberation, and ill-dignified, where the shock is resisted and turns into panic or damage. Ask what in your
situation was built on an assumption you have not looked at.

## How to read The Tower in a spread

In a [[spread:celtic-cross]] the position matters more than the card: in the "challenge" place it names what must
change; in the "outcome" place it asks whether you are ready to let go. For a first practice, try the
[[spread:three-card]] and draw for past, present and what needs to fall.
